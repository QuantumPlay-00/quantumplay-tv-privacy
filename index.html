01 · The short version

The TV app has no sign-up, no email or password, no ads and no analytics. Your videos are played on your devices and are not uploaded to us. Data leaves the TV only for the features listed in section 02.

02 · What the TV app does with data

Anonymous sign-in

When the app starts (after you accept this notice), it signs in to Firebase Authentication anonymously. This creates a random identifier for this TV. It is not tied to your name or email.

Linking a phone

When you link a phone, a 6-digit pairing code, this TV's model name, this TV's random identifier and the link between this TV and your QuantumPlay account are stored in Firebase Realtime Database. The TV then shows your account's display name and profile picture. While the TV shows its pairing screen, it also announces itself on your local network so your phone can find it. You can unlink at any time from Settings on the TV or from the phone app; unlinking removes the link on both sides.

Library and continue watching

The TV reads the library your phone synced to your account (titles, artwork links and progress). When you watch something on the TV, it writes your playback position, the TV's name and the playing state back to your account so you can resume on either device. Your theme choice is also read from your account.

Watch Party

If you join a Watch Party, this TV's name, your chat messages, playback state and connection set-up data are exchanged with the host and the other people in the party through Firebase. The video itself travels directly between devices over WebRTC. The TV app does not record audio or video.

Phone link over the internet

If you use the optional internet link to your phone, connection set-up data passes through Firebase, and the media travels over a WebRTC connection between your devices.

Connection helpers (STUN / TURN)

To set up WebRTC connections, the TV contacts Google's public STUN servers (stun.l.google.com) and a public relay (TURN) service run by Metered (openrelay.metered.ca). These services can see this TV's IP address, and the relay can carry encrypted connection traffic when a direct connection is not possible.

Titles and artwork

To show posters, backdrops and details, the TV app sends title searches to TMDB (The Movie Database). The search text is derived from your video file names, and TMDB receives this TV's IP address. Personal videos that have no match are simply shown without artwork. To get around network blocks on TMDB, the TV looks up TMDB's address through Cloudflare's DNS-over-HTTPS service (cloudflare-dns.com), so Cloudflare can see that this TV looked up TMDB's address. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

Network sources

If you add a network source (SMB, WebDAV or similar), its address, username and password are saved on this TV only. The password is encrypted with a key kept in the Android Keystore. They are sent only to the server you chose.

Files, storage and permissions

The TV app asks for access to videos on the TV and on connected drives so it can list and play them. Files are read on the device and are not uploaded to us. It also asks to show notifications for playback and to keep the device awake while a video plays.

What the TV app does not use

Advertising, analytics, crash-reporting services, camera, microphone, contacts and location. The TV app does not use them.

03 · Information we process

Because the TV app has no registration, we do not ask for your name, email address or password in the TV app. The information involved is:

  • A random anonymous identifier for this TV (Firebase).
  • This TV's model name and the pairing code while linking.
  • The display name and profile picture of the QuantumPlay account you link (set in the phone app).
  • Your synced library entries and playback progress.
  • Watch Party names, chat messages and playback state while you are in a party.
  • Technical data that any internet connection reveals, such as this TV's IP address, to the services in section 04.

We do not knowingly process sensitive categories of personal information in the TV app, and we do not collect information about you from other sources.

04 · Who receives data

We do not sell your information. The services the TV app talks to are:

  • Google Firebase (Authentication and Realtime Database, hosted on Google Cloud): anonymous sign-in, pairing, library and progress sync, Watch Party and phone-link signaling.
  • TMDB: title searches and artwork (receives the search text and IP address).
  • Cloudflare: DNS-over-HTTPS lookups for TMDB's address.
  • Google (STUN) and Metered Open Relay (TURN): WebRTC connection set-up.
  • Servers you choose yourself: SMB / WebDAV or similar network sources.

These providers have their own privacy policies. We may also share information if the law requires it, or in a merger or sale of our business.

05 · Cookies, tracking and advertising

The TV app does not use advertising or analytics trackers and does not follow you across other apps or websites. It does not respond to Do-Not-Track signals because it does not track. Normal device storage (app settings, a cache of artwork) is used to make the app work.

06 · International transfers

Our Firebase database is hosted in Asia (Singapore region). Your information may be processed in other countries by the services in section 04, including the United States. If you live in the EEA, UK or Switzerland, this means your information may be transferred outside your region.

07 · How long we keep information

Pairing codes are removed when pairing finishes. The link between your TV and account is kept until you unlink. Library and progress data stay in your QuantumPlay account until you change them or ask us to delete them. Settings stored on the TV stay until you clear the app's data or uninstall it. Watch Party messages and set-up data are only needed while a party is running. You can ask us to delete your data at any time (section 10).

08 · Security

We use technical and organizational measures to protect information, including encrypted connections to Firebase and TMDB and Keystore encryption for network-source passwords. Some network sources you add yourself (for example plain FTP or WebDAV over http) may not be encrypted by the server you choose. No system can be guaranteed 100% secure.
